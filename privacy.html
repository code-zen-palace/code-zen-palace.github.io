Tiny Acres Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 31, 2026. Operator: PrimeKamp LLC, 1209 Mountain Road Pl NE, Ste N, Albuquerque, NM 87110, USA, hello@primekampllc.com ("we").

Tiny Acres is a puzzle game for Android and iPhone, and the day's puzzle can also be played in a web browser at primekampllc.com/tiny-acres/play/ (the "web version", section 14). This policy explains what information the app and the web version handle, why, and your choices. We designed the game to need as little data as possible: there are no accounts, no chat, no location access, and we never receive your game progress: it stays on your device (and, if you use Android's own backup, in your own Google account backup, see section 1; if you sign in to Google Play Games, in your Play Games saved games, see section 5; on iPhone, see section 15).

1. Information the app stores on your device

Your puzzle results, streak, in-progress games, settings and whether you own Tiny Acres Plus are saved in a single file inside the app's private storage on your device. We never receive this file and cannot see it. Deleting it is under your control: Settings → "Delete my data", or uninstall the app.

Android backup. If backup is turned on for your phone (Android Settings → Google → Backup), Android may include this file in your own Google account backup, so your progress comes back if you reinstall the game or move to a new phone. That backup is made by Android and kept by Google under your Google account; we have no access to it. You can turn backup off, or delete backups, in your phone's or Google account's backup settings.

2. Advertising (free version only)

The free version shows a small number of ads through Google AdMob (Google LLC). To do this, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects your device's advertising identifier and app set ID, your IP address (which may be used to estimate your general location), interactions such as app launches, taps and ad views, and performance diagnostics such as app launch time. Google uses this data for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and all of it is encrypted in transit. Google uses this data as described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and the AdMob data disclosure. The app does not request your device's location and we cap ad content at the "PG" rating.

Your choices. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland the app asks for your consent before loading ads, using Google's consent framework; you can change your choice at any time in Settings → "Ad privacy options". Elsewhere, you can limit ad personalisation in your Android settings (Settings → Google → Ads) and you can opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information under US state laws by using the same in-app privacy options where offered. Buying Tiny Acres Plus removes all ads; from the next time you open the app, the ads SDK is not started at all.

3. Reminders and ratings

If you turn on the optional daily reminder, Tiny Acres schedules notifications on your device. Nothing about them is sent to us or anyone else. Turn them off at any time in the app's Settings or in Android's notification settings. The app may also show Google Play's own "rate this game" window once; anything you write there is handled by Google Play under its own policies.

4. Purchases

Tiny Acres Plus is a one-time purchase processed by Google Play. Google provides us with a purchase token and product identifier so the app can unlock Plus; Google's handling of payment data is governed by the Google Privacy Policy. We do not receive your payment details.

5. Google Play Games (optional)

You can play without it. If you choose to sign in to Google Play Games, Tiny Acres sends Google your scores for its leaderboards: today's acre as a percentage of its target, your longest streak and your best Free play score. Google shows your Play Games name and picture next to your scores to other players, as your Play Games profile settings allow. While you are signed in, Tiny Acres also keeps a copy of your game progress in your Play Games saved games, so that it can come back on a new phone: your puzzle results and the tiles you placed, your streak, your Free play scores, your landmarks and any puzzle you are part-way through. It does not include your settings or your Tiny Acres Plus purchase. The copy is stored by Google in your Play Games account, not by us: we never receive it, and only Tiny Acres, on a device signed in to your account, can read it. It is updated after each puzzle you finish and when you leave the app. Using "Delete my data" in the app while signed in deletes this copy too. Google Play Games Services also collects diagnostics and analytics about the service itself. This data is handled by Google under the Google Privacy Policy and is sent encrypted. We do not receive your email address and we do not use this data for advertising. You can sign out, change who sees your profile, or delete your Play Games data, including this copy, at any time in your Play Games profile or your Google Account.

6. Crash and performance data

When the app crashes or hits a serious error, it sends a crash report to Firebase Crashlytics (Google LLC) so we can find and fix the problem. A report contains the technical details of the error (the code location and error message), the app version, your phone's model, Android version and free memory and storage at the time, and a random Crashlytics installation ID that is not linked to you and changes if you reinstall the app. It contains no game progress, no name, no email and no advertising ID. We use these reports only to keep the game stable; they are kept for 90 days and then deleted by Crashlytics. See Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Google Play may also collect anonymised crash and performance statistics (Android vitals) from devices whose owners have opted into usage and diagnostics sharing, and shares only aggregate reports with us. The ads SDK described in section 2 also collects performance diagnostics such as app launch time.

7. Analytics

The app does not include a separate analytics service. Google AdMob may use the information described in section 2 for analytics about ad performance. If we add an analytics service, we will update this policy before that version is released.

8. Children

Tiny Acres is not directed to children under 13 (or the applicable age of digital consent in your country) and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. The app is listed for ages 13 and over. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the ads SDK, contact us and we will help you have it removed.

9. Data sharing and sale

We do not sell personal information. The only third parties that receive data from the app are Google (ads, billing, crash reports, platform diagnostics and, if you sign in to Play Games, your leaderboard scores, Play Games name and saved game progress) as described above. For the web version, see section 14. On iPhone, Apple handles the App Store's rating prompt (section 15). We do not share data with data brokers.

11. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example the EU/UK GDPR, California CCPA/CPRA, Brazil LGPD, India DPDP), you may have rights to access, correct, delete or port personal information, to object to or restrict processing, to withdraw consent, and to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority. Because we hold no personal information about you ourselves, most requests are fulfilled by the in-app controls above; for anything else, email hello@primekampllc.com and we will respond within 30 days. We do not discriminate against you for exercising your rights.

12. International transfers

Google processes data in the United States and other countries under its own safeguards (including the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses).

13. Security

All network traffic from the app uses HTTPS. The save file lives in the app's sandboxed private storage. The web version is served only over HTTPS, and its save is readable only by pages on primekampllc.com in your browser.

14. The web version

You can play the day's puzzle in a web browser at primekampllc.com/tiny-acres/play/, the page that shared results link to. The web version has no ads, no purchases, no accounts or sign-in, no notifications, no analytics and no crash reporting, and it sets no cookies.

What it keeps in your browser. Your results, your streak, the puzzle you are playing and your settings (such as sound) are saved in your browser's local storage for primekampllc.com, under the name tiny_acres_save. They stay in that browser on that device: we never receive them, and they are separate from the app's progress on your phone. To delete them, use "Delete my data" at the foot of the game's page, or clear the site data for primekampllc.com in your browser's settings. In a private or incognito window they are deleted when you close the window.

What the services that deliver the page see. As with any web page, your browser sends your IP address and browser details to the services that deliver it: GitHub, which hosts primekampllc.com and logs visitors' IP addresses for security (see GitHub Pages data collection and the GitHub Privacy Statement), and Google, whose servers (www.gstatic.com and fonts.gstatic.com) deliver the game's drawing engine and its fonts (see the Google Privacy Policy). We do not receive this information from them, and the page runs no advertising or tracking code.

Sharing and links. Share uses your browser's own share menu, or copies your result for you to paste; only the text and picture you choose to share go to the app you choose. The "Get the app" and "Get Tiny Acres on Google Play" buttons open Tiny Acres on Google Play with a campaign tag that says the visit came from the web version (nothing about you), so that Google Play's reports can count how many installs it brings.

What a shared link carries. A result shared from the app or from this page links here, and the link carries the shared acre itself: its tile placements, and with them its score, written after the "#" at the end of the address. Browsers never send that part of an address to a server. Only the game's page in the recipient's browser reads it, to show the two acres side by side; it is never sent to us, and the page does not keep it. Like the rest of a shared message, the link is seen by the app you share it with and by the people you send it to.

15. Tiny Acres on iPhone

Tiny Acres on iPhone works as described above, with these differences.

Advertising and tracking. The free version shows ads from Google AdMob as described in section 2. On iPhone, Google's ads SDK uses your device's advertising identifier (IDFA) to personalise and measure ads only if you allow it in Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt. The prompt appears, after a short explanation from Google, the first time an ad could be shown (when you open Free play or ask for an extra undo with a video), not when the app starts. If you choose "Ask App Not to Track", the advertising identifier is not used and ads are not personalised with it; the game is the same either way. You can change your choice at any time in the Settings app under Privacy & Security › Tracking. Whatever you choose, the ads SDK collects the data described in section 2 (such as your IP address, from which an approximate location may be estimated, how you interact with ads, and diagnostics) to show and measure ads and to prevent fraud. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form appears first, as in section 2.

Purchases. The iPhone version currently offers no purchases.

Ratings. The app may show Apple's own rating prompt once, and "Rate Tiny Acres" in Settings opens the App Store. Anything you write there is handled by Apple.

Reminders. Notifications are scheduled on your iPhone; iOS asks your permission when you turn them on, and you can turn them off in the app or in the Settings app.

No Game Center, no cloud save. The iPhone version does not use Game Center, leaderboards or any cloud save: section 5 (Google Play Games) does not apply to it.

Crash reports. Firebase Crashlytics works on iPhone as described in section 6. If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in the Settings app, Apple may also give us aggregated crash and usage statistics.

Your progress. Your game progress stays in the app's storage on your iPhone. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or a computer, the backup includes it; that backup is yours, kept by Apple or on your computer, and we cannot access it. "Delete my data" in Settings, or deleting the app, erases it from the iPhone.

Photos. If you choose "Save Image" when sharing a picture, iOS asks your permission to add it to your photo library. The app never reads your photos.
